==========================================================================================================================
DENOMINATOR SENSITIVITY -- the two arms do not filter equivalent mutants the same way
==========================================================================================================================
Arm A can tell an equivalent mutant from a real one for free: Halide
compiles in stages, so the mutated generator's emitted .stmt can be
diffed against the baseline's before any test runs. 200 of its 1,983
points corpus-wide are provably equivalent at target=host and 51 are
killed by the Halide compiler itself. Arm C has no such stage -- the
emitted .cpp already is the lowered pipeline -- so nothing distinguishes
an equivalent emitted-C++ mutant from a live one short of running it.
That asymmetry is itself a property of multi-stage DSL compilation, but
it means arm A's rate is over a cleaner denominator, so both are shown.

arm A, over all evaluated (incl. equivalent)    735      95     398    12.9%    54.1%
